## Abstract / 摘要

Text embedding models are central to information retrieval and deep research tasks, and their behaviors are strongly shaped by the training objective. While recent systems have largely converged on InfoNCE-style loss functions, there have been continued attempts to design additional loss functions beyond this core objective. One such component is spread-out loss, a batch-level penalty on high pairwise similarity that encourages embeddings to occupy the representation space more evenly. However, in existing systems, this design is usually combined with many other design choices and has not been sufficiently ablated. To isolate its effect, we use spread-out loss as a controlled lens for studying geometric regularization in text embedding training. Across 13 retrieval benchmarks, spread-out improves fine-tuned retrievers over contrastive baselines across architectures and model sizes. We then diagnose where these gains arise and find that they concentrate on short queries, where spread-out reduces query-side directional crowding. Based on this diagnosis, we propose QUARTO, a Query-length Adaptive spread-out loss that uses batch composition to scale the full regularizer more strongly for short-query-dominated batches and less strongly for longer-query-dominated batches. QUARTO further improves retrieval performance over fixed-weight spread-out. Overall, our findings point to the potential of embedding-space regularization and suggest that text-embedding training should explore a broader design space of objectives beyond InfoNCE-style contrastive learning.
