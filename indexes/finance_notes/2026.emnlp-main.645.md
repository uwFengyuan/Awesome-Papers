## Abstract / 摘要

When an aligned language model declines a request, it almost always states a reason. Those reasons are read as evidence about the refusal, yet whether they name what actually triggered it has not been measured. We introduce a counterfactual audit of the cited reason, removing the reason-bearing feature to test necessity and inserting it into a benign prompt to test sufficiency. The two tests partition cited reasons into four kinds: faithful, over-cited, under-specified, and confabulated. We audit 22 models from nine developers on 840 prompts pooled from seven public safety benchmarks. Across the audited refusals, 57.2% cite a reason that fails at least one test, though faithful remains the largest category for 21 of 22 models. Over-citation is highest in the models that refuse most broadly. Paired sweeps over two families’ post-training checkpoints place its rise at supervised fine-tuning, not at later preference optimization. Probes on open-weight models tie these labels to internal representations. Category rates hold under a different-family judge, and the faithful-versus-over-cited ordering holds under an own-reason audit. We release the framework, the counterfactual variants, and per-model labels.
