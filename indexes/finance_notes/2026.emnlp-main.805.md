## Abstract / 摘要

The recent advancement of time series has witnessed Vision Language Models (VLMs) as a powerful tool for a variety of tasks of multimodal time series analysis, including forecasting, classification, question answering, etc. However, with time series as an explicit modality, inputs with long time series horizons yield excessive numeric tokens for VLMs, resulting in high inference latency. Moreover, such long contexts often contain redundant or noisy segments that might distract the model and thus degrade downstream performance. Although previous token pruning methods can reduce visual or textual tokens, they are mostly unimodal and do not explicitly explore multimodal semantics for time series. To address this, we propose TimePrune, a plug-and-play temporal pruning framework for VLMs in multimodal time series analysis, which can accelerate VLM inference without fine-tuning the VLM backbone. Specifically, TimePrune formulates time series pruning as a budgeted temporal selection problem and introduces a Trimodal Budgeted Network to score temporal patches via jointly conditioning on linguistic and visual semantics. Then, under a fixed budget, this framework is optimized via the proposed Narrative-Calibrated Binned Grounding constraints, to further facilitate context-aware and temporally-coherent pruning. Extensive experiments in four time series tasks across application domains on multiple VLM backbones show that TimePrune could consistently achieve better performances. For example, on Qwen3VL-8B, TimePrune improves average forecasting performance by 33.91%.
