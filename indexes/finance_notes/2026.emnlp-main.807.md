## Abstract / 摘要

Multimodal large language models have recently shown strong potential for time series reasoning, where models jointly process textual context and long numerical sequences. However, dense time series representations introduce a large number of tokens, leading to substantial computational and memory overhead during inference. Existing token pruning methods are mainly designed for visual tokens and typically rely on global attention or representation geometry, overlooking the fact that token redundancy varies across layers and modalities. This makes pruning-induced perturbations difficult to control, especially when tokens are pruned across multiple transformer layers. In this paper, we propose StratPrune, a cascade stratum pruning framework for efficient time series reasoning. StratPrune first identifies an early-layer pruning stratum where time series tokens remain less diverse than text tokens, indicating high redundancy suitable for stable pruning. We then derive a hidden-state stability bound that decomposes pruning-induced perturbation into a layer-dependent stability term and a token-level attention saliency term. Guided by this analysis, StratPrune allocates layer-wise pruning budgets according to time series diversity and removes low-saliency time series tokens through a context-aware cascade process. Experiments on 7 tasks show that StratPrune outperforms strong pruning baselines under matched token budgets, achieving up to 30% relative improvement.
